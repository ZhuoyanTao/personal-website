I'm Zhuoyan Tao, Terry to friends and colleagues. I'm a master's student in CMU's Intelligent Information Systems program at the Language Technologies Institute, advised by Prof. Shinji Watanabe at WavLab.

My work sits at the boundary of signal processing, machine learning, and linguistics, and keeps circling one question: what listeners take from speech beyond the words, and how to measure and recover it when the signal is partial, degraded, or switching between languages. Right now I'm the lead student researcher on target-speaker speech restoration with Amazon AGI.

Research

Current focus · CMU WavLab × Amazon AGI

Target-Speaker Speech Restoration

Two people on a damaged phone call: recover just one of them, cleanly, given a few seconds of their voice. It is a generative problem with an unforgiving failure mode, restoring the wrong speaker.

  • Lead student researcher; models trained on 7.8K two-party calls.
  • Built the evaluation suite: word error rate (WER, and permutation-invariant cpWER for multi-speaker audio), speaker similarity, wrong-speaker rate, VERSA quality metrics, and paired-bootstrap significance tests. Benchmarked 9 external systems.
  • Made encoder training 1.20× faster with 26% less memory by moving to fused scaled-dot-product attention (SDPA) with a custom backward pass for the position bias.
  • Generative restoration
  • Speaker conditioning
  • Evaluation
  • PyTorch · ESPnet
CMU WavLab

Perceptual Speech Quality

Predicting how listeners would rate speech from partial audio, with no clean reference, for streaming and generative systems. ANCHOR cut PLCMOS error by 21–35% on 2-second prefixes and located a 4–6 s perceptual context horizon. Since then: a ~20× faster one-pass variant at equal accuracy, a zero-shot A/B judge within 0.01 Spearman correlation of the top VoiceMOS 2026 entry on URGENT 2026 pairs, and best-of-N TTS sample selection from the first 2 seconds.

Multilingual & Code-Switched Speech

Spoken language diarization labels which language is spoken when, which code-switching conversation needs. I study what pre-trained encoders (Whisper, MMS) already know about language identity, and built off-target posterior mass, a training-free diagnostic that forecasts when fine-tuning a lightweight probe will pay off.

Peer-reviewed

ANCHOR: Autoregressive Non-intrusive Chunk-Ordered Refinement for Joint Multi-Resolution Speech Quality Modeling

Zhuoyan Tao, Jiatong Shi, Hye-jin Shim, Shinji Watanabe
Proc. Interspeech 2026, pp. 5672–5677

Predicts how listeners will rate speech before the utterance finishes, with 21–35% lower PLCMOS error than ARECHO on 2-second prefixes.

Streaming and generative systems need quality estimates from partial audio, but existing predictors assume the full utterance. Extending ARECHO (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight), ANCHOR models chunk- and utterance-level quality in one autoregressive decoder with dual-resolution query tokens, and shows that judgments converge after a 4–6 s perceptual context horizon.

Correction The published version reports 48%. We later found a resampling bug in the toolkit that labelled the baseline's PLCMOS training data; with corrected labels the gain is 21–35%. UTMOS, DNSMOS, and NISQA results and the 4–6 s horizon are unaffected. Erratum in preparation.

TurnBench: A Multi-Domain Benchmark for Turn-Taking Dynamics in Spoken Dialogue

Freeman Jiang, Ramon Sanabria, Soham Deshmukh, …, Zhuoyan Tao, …, Hung-yi Lee, Shinji Watanabe (19 authors)
Proc. IEEE Spoken Language Technology Workshop (SLT) 2026

No current turn-taking system matches humans, who start speaking a median 151 ms before a turn ends, without excessive false positives.

Thirty hours of hand-labeled, triple-annotated dyadic conversation across six interaction styles, a standardized protocol for end-of-turn and interruption detection, 14 benchmarked systems, and a released 104-hour training set and public leaderboard.

My contribution Helped design the evaluation protocol and submitted an ANCHOR-based model as an initial baseline.

Under review

Off-target posterior mass: A training-free diagnostic for fine-tuning spoken language diarization probes

Zhuoyan Tao, Aditya Kommineni, Shrikanth Narayanan
Submitted to JASA Express Letters · arXiv:2611.13430

A single training-free number, computed from language-ID posteriors, that forecasts whether fine-tuning a diarization probe will pay off.

Off-target posterior mass (OTPM) correlates with probe gains at 0.56 on unseen synthetic bilingual data and 0.23 on real-world corpora (DISPLACE; MUCS Hindi–English code-switching). The evaluation was pre-registered on unseen language pairs and run across Whisper, MMS, and classical language-ID encoders.
